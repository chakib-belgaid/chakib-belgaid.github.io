BP Log↓

BP Log

Personal project

Can a photo save some typing?

A blood-pressure reading should be easy to keep. BP Log reads the monitor from a photo, lets the user check the values, and saves them on the phone, offline.

Keep the system small

Image-processing rules handle the display’s layout; a small neural network, trained only on generated digits, reads the digits in plain JavaScript. When a photo failed, I fixed that failure. A heavier runtime would not have solved it.

Whisperbook↓

Whisperbook

Personal project

Can a phone narrate a book without uploading it?

I wanted to enjoy books while walking or before sleep. The options I tried felt expensive or sounded robotic. A book can stay yours, even when it speaks.

Enforce the promise in the app

Whisperbook narrates EPUBs and PDFs on the phone, with character voices and synchronized text. Its manifest removes the network permission, so the promise does not rest on my word. Audio is prepared progressively: the opening chapter can play while the rest is still being narrated.

Energy tools↓

pyRAPL, pyJoules & Wattch

Research & independent tools

What can an energy trace actually tell us?

Know what you measured before you optimize it. I designed pyRAPL to examine energy use during Python experiments. GPU and other measurement sources led us to pyJoules.

Keep the measurement inspectable

Restricted access and work on macOS led to Wattch: separate collection, evaluation and reporting, with a simple workflow and raw traces developers can inspect.

Measured

Wattch’s overhead against a native RAPL reader: no reproducible comparison is linked

Wattch case→pyRAPL source↗ (opens in a new tab)pyJoules source↗ (opens in a new tab)

I designed both Python libraries and developed them with the research team. Wattch’s Rust implementation is AI-assisted. Hardware readings cover device activity during an interval; they do not automatically isolate one function’s energy.

At MCQ Scan↓

MCQ Scan

Lead AI Engineer · 2024–present

Who decides what an agent may do?

A client’s cloud. A business rule. A tool an agent must not call. Here the constraints are not mine to choose.

For Store Chat, a clothing-shopping assistant, I evaluated constrained-decoding tools and integrated LM Format Enforcer with vLLM to enforce allowed enum values, alongside Pydantic validation for business coherence. For a client that required hosting in its own Google Cloud, I implemented compiled application packaging for serverless deployment, so we delivered without distributing the source.

Check the permission where the tool runs

I conceived AgentDiet, an agent-governance project: signed Biscuit tokens, delegations that can only narrow, and local checks before a tool is used. I built the Python/FastAPI demonstrator and prepared the technical proposal, market analysis, budget and project plan. It received a 2026 Hyper Open X grant, funded by France 2030.

Details

Data transfer: reported bytes × 0.3 kWh/GB × 494 g CO₂e/kWh, computed with co2.js using the Sustainable Web Design Model v4, which includes operational and embodied estimates for data centres, networks, and devices. Unknown sizes are excluded, and cached resources add no reported network bytes.

Rendering and animation: the main-thread time spent updating and drawing the garden and sky at an assumed 10 W, plus the GPU time of each garden frame at an assumed 20 W, × 473 g CO₂e/kWh, co2.js’s world average grid intensity. GPU time is counted only where the browser exposes GPU timers; elsewhere this part is a CPU-only lower bound. Browsers report time, not power, so the wattages are assumptions, and the model’s device share of transfer may overlap a little with this measured rendering.

Garden sounds, when on, are made by the browser’s audio engine. Their small cost isn’t measured here.
